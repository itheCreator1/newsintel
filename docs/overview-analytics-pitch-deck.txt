Slide 1: From Stub to Signal: Turning the Overview Page Into a Real Intelligence Dashboard. Two features turn the first screen an analyst opens into an answer, not a status light. One tracks how much is coming in and when it spikes. The other tracks what it is about, by entity and by country. Both drill straight into the same search the rest of the product already uses. Speaker note: Everything in this deck reuses two things NewsIntel already built, so what is actually new here is small and well scoped.

Slide 2: The Overview page today is one status card, and two urgent questions go unanswered. It shows only core services health. Operational data exists, but it is scattered across the Jobs, Sources, and Search pages. Nobody can see ingestion volume or a notable spike at a glance. Nobody can see what the archive is currently about, by entity or by country. The product spec already requires the overview to show ingestion rate, notable spikes, and top entities and countries. Speaker note: This is not a new ask. It is already in the spec; we are closing the gap between what is specified and what is built.

Slide 3: Feature 1: an ingestion timeline that flags spikes automatically. It is a 30 day bar chart of articles ingested per day. A spike detector flags any day at or above two times the trailing 7 day average. Flagged days are visually distinct, so a surge reads instantly. The chart reads Article.first_discovered_at directly from PostgreSQL. Speaker note: Because it reads Postgres rather than Elasticsearch, this chart keeps working during a search outage, so it becomes a status signal in its own right.

Slide 4: Every bar is a doorway into the archive, not just a count. Clicking any day opens a date filtered search scoped to that day's articles. Clicking a flagged spike does the same, straight into the coverage that caused it. This reuses the product's existing URL driven investigation state, the same mechanism the Search page already runs on. No new filtering logic is introduced to make this work. Speaker note: An analyst never has to leave the chart to start investigating; the click is the transition from noticing to searching.

Slide 5: Feature 2: top entities and top countries, side by side. Two horizontal bar charts sit over a rolling window. The left panel shows the top 10 named entities, filterable by type such as PERSON or ORG. The right panel shows the top primary story countries by article volume. Together they answer what the archive is about right now. Speaker note: This is the entity and country panel the product spec already calls for on the overview; we are building the scope that is on paper, not inventing new scope.

Slide 6: Clicking a bar starts a pre-filtered search, not a dead end. Clicking an entity opens a search pre-filtered to every article mentioning that entity. Clicking a country opens a search pre-filtered to every article whose primary story country is that country. Both drill-downs use the same click-to-search pattern as the ingestion timeline. The entity panel's type filter narrows the bars themselves before any click happens. Speaker note: Once an analyst learns to click a chart on this page, they have learned it for both panels, since the interaction pattern is identical.

Slide 7: Together, the two panels form a daily analyst workflow, not two separate widgets. Start on the ingestion timeline and notice a flagged spike. Click into that day's articles. Cross-reference the top entities and countries panel to see who or where the spike is about. Click an entity or country bar to narrow the search further. Speaker note: The two features were built to hand off into each other; the timeline answers when, the panel answers who and where.

Slide 8: We are not duplicating the Jobs page; we are answering questions it does not. Stat counters, feed health, and failed jobs already live on the Jobs page. These two features add ingestion trend and anomaly detection, plus topical composition, neither of which exists anywhere today. The product spec's overview requirements name exactly this: ingestion rate, notable spikes, and top entities and countries. Nothing proposed here is a second version of an existing chart. Speaker note: The pitch is not more dashboard; it is the two specific answers this page is missing, and no scattered Jobs-page counter already covers them.

Slide 9: Both features are built from parts the product already has. The chart panels reuse the existing ECharts timeline component. Drill-down search reuses the existing investigation-state URL helpers. The only new backend surface is three PostgreSQL aggregation endpoints under a new analytics domain. The spike-detection logic is a small, pure, unit-tested helper function. Speaker note: Because the hard parts, charting and URL-state search, are already proven in production, the genuinely new surface area is small and easy to test in isolation.

Slide 10: This milestone turns the Overview into the place investigations start. An analytics backend domain exists for the first time, built specifically for these aggregation endpoints. The click-to-search pattern is now proven against two more chart types beyond the original search timeline. The overview page becomes a natural first stop for an investigation, not just a health check. The pure spike-detection helper is unit tested on its own, independent of the UI it feeds. Speaker note: We are not just shipping two charts; we are standing up the analytics domain and the drill-down pattern that the next overview feature will build into.

Slide 11: Next steps: three endpoints, two panels, one helper function. Build the three PostgreSQL aggregation endpoints under the new analytics backend domain. Build the two chart panels using the existing ECharts timeline component and investigation-state URL helpers. Implement the spike-detection logic as a pure, unit-tested helper. Wire click-through drill-downs into the existing search page for both panels. Speaker note: There is no open design question left here; this is scoped down to implementation, which is why we can commit to it now.
